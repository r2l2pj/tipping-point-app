## Theme sentence

> Tipping Point turns NASA rainfall and soil moisture data into a near real-time landslide and flood warning, then uses NISAR radar to confirm where the ground actually moved or flooded, so civil defense teams know where to act first.

## Summary (~165 words)

In May 2024, Rio Grande do Sul suffered the worst floods in state history — 446 of 497 municipalities hit, hundreds of thousands displaced. Alerts covered entire municipalities, and optical satellites were blind behind storm clouds: in the critical first hours, no one knew which slopes had failed or which neighborhoods were under water. Tipping Point closes that gap. Our Tipping Point Index (TPI) is the geometric mean of three normalized scores in [0,1] — Rain (GPM IMERG vs per-pixel climatology), Soil (SMAP root-zone moisture), Terrain (NASADEM slope and drainage height) — classified Low (<0.2), Moderate, High, or Critical (>0.6). The 2024 flood predates NISAR, so radar confirmation is demonstrated on a second case: in Washington State, December 2025, NISAR GCOV passes before and after the storm confirm flooded area through clouds and darkness, while landslide sites appear only as "candidates" from post-event HLS optical imagery. Built for municipal Civil Defense in Southern Brazil (RS/SC/PR), the demo replays both events end-to-end; the live operational pipeline is on our roadmap. Tipping Point would have warned X hours earlier. In replay, using only data available at the time, the index crossed Critical a median of X h (IQR A-B h) before mapped landslides (n = N).
